The Problem

The Die Tryout Problem

Why tool rooms discover their mistakes only after cutting steel

What goes wrong today
  • The die is cut first and the problems show up during tryout — every loop costs weeks of machine time and rework.
  • Skilled die setters are scarce; tribal knowledge lives in heads and paper notes, not in checkable rules.
  • Blank estimation, thinning and springback are hand-calculated — one wrong inner radius scraps a complete die.
  • Quotes are gut-feel: strip nesting, sheet plan, material and tool cost guessed under deadline pressure.
  • Tryout learnings are lost — the same mistake is paid for again on the next die.

Under the hood

A Rules Engine You Can Audit

Pure functions, real sheet-metal mechanics, parameters injected — nothing hard-coded

backend/app/rules/blanking.py
rules engine · pure functions, no web code
import math def blanking_force_kn(perimeter_mm, thickness_mm, shear_strength_mpa): """ Cutting (blanking) force: F = L x t x tau L = cut perimeter in mm, t = sheet thickness in mm, tau = shear strength in MPa (N/mm^2). Returns force in kN. """ return perimeter_mm * thickness_mm * shear_strength_mpa / 1000.0 def press_tonnage(force_kn, safety_factor): """ Press tonnage from force in kN: tonnes = kN / 9.81, then times the safety factor from the parameters table. """ return force_kn / 9.81 * safety_factor

Calibration in three steps
Record — tryout measurements: thinning 23.1% vs predicted 18.4%, springback 2.8° vs 2.2°.
Propose — advisor suggests correction factor × 1.04 for this workshop.
Confirm — applies to your workshop only; predicted-vs-actual ledger updates.
Applied AI, constrained
  • Defect photo diagnosis on any OpenAI-compatible endpoint — NVIDIA NIM Llama-3.2-11B-Vision as the tested default.
  • Constrained AI: fixed defect list, confidence score, allowed to say unsure — never invents numbers.
  • Component-drawing reader and AI report summary — engine numbers only, flagged for human check.
  • Die history timeline and consent flag for the improvement dataset keep every verdict auditable after production.
  • Degrades honestly: without credentials the vision features report switched off — the rules engine still runs.
Die-change ledger

Does the Rework Actually Work?

The tool-room loop everyone runs — now recorded, and it actually closes

The loop, written down
  • Trial N shows a defect, the die is reworked, trial N+1 shows whether the rework worked — the loop every tool room already runs, usually in a notebook.
  • Record what was done to the die after a trial; the next trial that records what was seen closes it out — resolved, no help, or still pending.
  • A blank trial sheet proves nothing (not inspected yet), so the change stays pending — outcomes compare two trial sheets and nothing more.
  • Per-defect roll-up across the job — which rework fixes which defect on which die — and the suggestion path records the deterministic cause table as engine_suggestion.
Why this is the moat
Resolved The defect it targeted is absent from the next trial's sheet — and other defects were recorded, so the sheet is not simply empty.
No help The same defect is still there. Recorded as knowledge, not failure — a change that did not work is exactly as valuable as one that did.
Recorded, not judged
The toolmaker's description is kept as written; the outcome is only the comparison of two trial sheets. AutoForm never touches the tryout bench — this dataset starts here.
Machine connectivity

The Closed Loop: The Press Talks Back

Predict the die, watch it run on the press, and calibrate from every stroke

One stroke, end to end
  • Register — a press joins the shop list and mints a machine token: owner-only, hashed at rest, shown once.
  • Stream — a gateway beside the machine pushes strokes over OPC-UA, Modbus TCP or MQTT — or the bundled simulator, zero hardware.
  • Judge — overload vs the rating, headroom vs the 80% capacity parameter, drift vs the reference force and the rolling median of the last 20 strokes.
  • Learn — sustained drift proposes a safety-factor calibration; the owner confirms it and future tonnage estimates use it.

Safe by construction
  • The machine token authorizes one action — posting this press's strokes — and is stored only as a sha256 hash. Rotate it and the old one dies.
  • The gateway only pushes data outbound to the API. The press network is never exposed, and unplugged servers change nothing on the floor.
  • Advisory only: every stroke verdict is a risk level flagged for human check — it never approves a job, drives a press or stops one.
Where this goes next
The die tryout is the instrumented moment: the press is watched, the engine already predicted the force, and every measured stroke tightens the shop's calibration. Production monitoring and stroke-count tool life then come almost for free — the closed loop AutoForm cannot close.
CAD bend recognition

The CAD Already Knows the Bends

Read candidates from the STEP model — confirm them, never trust them blind

What the model gives for free
  • A bent sheet carries its bends as cylindrical faces: the inner cylinder's radius is the bend radius, the arc sweep is the bend angle.
  • The concentric cylinders of one bend — inner and outer surface — merge into a single candidate: the one the die is made to.
  • Candidates, not facts: holes, bosses and cosmetic rounds can appear too, so every one is flagged for the toolmaker to confirm.
  • The flats are not in the model — the toolmaker enters each leg from the drawing, then applies the table and the checks re-run.
Prediction travels on paper
  • The printable die-trial sheet carries the predicted thinning, springback and flat blank — the numbers this engine computed before metal was cut.
  • Against a measurement grid for the micrometer readings, a defect checklist, the die-change box and the signature line.
  • What was measured feeds the tryout screen afterwards — closing the same confirm-gated loop.
Honest limits, stated
B-spline bends, exotic STEP flavours and files with no cylinders return “no candidates — enter the table by hand” instead of a guess.
The workshop learns

Numbers the Shop Can Verify

Analytics, an advisor that answers from your own rows, and the surfaces a tool room runs on

Analytics & the advisor
  • Shop analytics: risk mix across every job, defect Pareto across every trial, die-change outcomes, press streams — computed from this workshop's own rows.
  • Ask the advisor: which jobs should I check first? did the die change help? Deterministic answers from shop data, and it says when a question is outside its range.
  • Design revisions: a new drawing becomes v2 of the same part — inputs copied, linked to its parent, and the previous revision's checks, quotes and trial history untouched.
  • Multi-bend parts checked bend by bend, and quote revisions priced side by side with their inputs kept beside each result.
The surfaces a tool room runs on
  • Workspace settings: your material grades (owner-only CRUD, everything still starting as an unverified candidate), the calibration ledger with confirm/reject, and the audit trail with filters.
  • CSV export of jobs, tryout trials and the die-change ledger — spreadsheet-injection defended — plus the printable die-trial sheet for the press.
Verifiable, not magic
Every tile, Pareto bar and answer is a query over rows the workshop can open, filter and export. Nothing on this screen needs the shop to trust a model.
